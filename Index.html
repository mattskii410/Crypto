    Signed into law

    GENIUS Act

    Signed July 18, 2025

    The first major US crypto law. It creates a federal framework for dollar-backed payment stablecoins: what counts as one, how they must be backed, and who can issue them. Banks, nonbanks and credit unions can now launch their own.

    Markets it touches

    Stablecoins (USDC, USDT, USD1)Circle (CRCL)Coinbase (COIN)Robinhood (HOOD)Banks

    Why it matters now: On Sep 24, 2026 the Federal Reserve proposed the rules that put the law into practice, including safe, liquid reserve requirements, redemption at par, and reserve disclosures. The public comment period is open, so the final details are still in play.

    DeFi broker rule repeal

    Signed April 2025

    Overturned a Biden-era IRS rule that would have made decentralized finance platforms report user transactions like traditional brokers. Critics said code-run protocols couldn't comply. It passed with bipartisan support.

    Not law yet

    CLARITY Act

    Stalled in Senate

    The big market-structure bill. It would split oversight of digital assets between the SEC and CFTC, and it allowed stablecoin companies to pay customers rewards. It passed the House 294 to 134 in July 2025, but on Sep 15, 2026 a Senate vote to open debate failed 49 to 50, short of the 60 needed.

    Banks, especially community banks, opposed the stablecoin rewards, arguing crypto firms could compete for deposits without bank-level rules. Ethics provisions tied to the president's own crypto ventures were another sticking point. With midterms approaching, many expect it to slip to 2027.

    What to watch

    1. Fed stablecoin rules. Stricter reserve rules could slow new stablecoin launches; looser ones could speed them.
    2. A CLARITY revival. Any new Senate deal would likely move exchange stocks and altcoins whose legal status is unclear.
    3. Fed rate decisions. Separate from crypto laws, rate-hike odds are moving Bitcoin and risk assets right now.

    Small-cap momentum playbook

    No one can reliably tell which small coins will rise; most small caps lose value over time, and past patterns often don't repeat. What you can do is use a clear rule set that finds coins showing momentum right now and limits how much you lose when it fades. That's what the screen on this tab does.

    What the screen looks for

    1. 01
      Small but real. Market cap between $30M and $1B, with at least $1M in daily volume, so there's room to grow but enough trading to get in and out.
    2. 02
      Volume surge. 24h volume above 25% of market cap. Unusual trading often comes before or alongside a move.
    3. 03
      Healthy momentum. Up 3% to 40% in 24 hours. Rising, but not already in a vertical spike.
    4. 04
      Trend confirmation. Also up over 7 days, so it's not just a one-day bounce inside a downtrend.

    Red flags that lower the score

    Up more than 40% in a day (you'd likely be buying from early sellers), thin volume (hard to exit), and a falling 7-day trend. Always check the project yourself for unlocked token supply, anonymous teams, and hype that's driving the move.

    Managing the trade

    1. Size small. Many traders cap any single small-cap position at 1 to 2% of their portfolio.
    2. Set a stop-loss before you buy, often 10 to 20% below entry, and don't move it down.
    3. Take profit in steps, like selling a portion at +25% and +50%, instead of waiting for a perfect top.
    4. Spread out across several positions; with small caps, a few winners usually have to cover several losers.
    5. Use limit orders to avoid slippage in thin markets.

    This is a trend screen, not a prediction or a recommendation to buy. I'm not a financial advisor, and small-cap crypto can drop 50% or more in a day. Only risk money you can afford to lose.

    Crypto basics

    What cryptocurrency is

    A cryptocurrency is digital money that runs on a blockchain: a shared public ledger copied across thousands of computers. No bank or government keeps the record. Instead, the network agrees on every transaction, and cryptography makes past entries practically impossible to change.

    You hold crypto through a wallet, which stores your private keys. Whoever controls the keys controls the coins, which is why people say "not your keys, not your coins."

    Coins vs. tokens

    Coins like Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) are native to their own blockchains. Tokens are built on top of an existing chain, such as the thousands of tokens on Ethereum or Solana. Stablecoins like USDC and USDT are tokens pegged to a currency, usually the US dollar, and are used to park value or move money between trades.

    What moves prices

    Supply and demand. Demand shifts with news, regulation, interest rates, big investors buying or selling, and overall market mood. Supply depends on each coin's rules; Bitcoin is capped at 21 million coins, while others have no cap. Smaller coins with low trading volume can swing wildly on relatively little money.

    How trading works

    1. Pick a platform. Most people use a centralized exchange (CEX) like Coinbase, Kraken or Binance, which requires identity verification. Decentralized exchanges (DEXs) let you trade straight from your own wallet.
    2. Fund your account. Deposit dollars or another currency by bank transfer or card, or send crypto in from a wallet.
    3. Place an order. A market order buys or sells right away at the current price. A limit order only fills at the price you set or better.
    4. Understand pairs. Every trade swaps one asset for another, shown as a pair like BTC/USD or ETH/BTC.
    5. Store it. Leave it on the exchange for convenience, or move it to your own wallet for more control.
    6. Track taxes. In many countries, selling, swapping, or spending crypto is a taxable event.

    Beyond buying and holding (spot trading), exchanges offer futures and leverage, which multiply both gains and losses and can wipe out a position quickly.

    Crypto is highly volatile and can lose most of its value fast. This page is general education, not financial advice.
